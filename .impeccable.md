## Design Context

### Users
Primary: 18–32 casual mobile gamers who are stock-market-curious but not active traders. Secondary: existing city-builder / hybrid-casual players (Monopoly GO-adjacent audience) looking for "more depth." Tertiary: finance-adjacent creators/communities (FinTok, casual investing subreddits). They're on their phones in the evening, scrolling social/app stores, drawn in by a hook — not doing deep financial research. This landing page's only job is to convert a scroll-by into an email signup before the app exists.

### Brand Personality
Stockio in three words: **confident, cinematic, rewarding.** It's a game that treats the stock market as spectacle — real stakes without real money. The tone should feel like a premium hybrid-casual game trailer meets a private members' club, not a fintech dashboard and not a cartoon mobile game icon. Never childish, never spreadsheet-dry.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Palette: **Coco Noir** (deep espresso/near-black, warm-tinted neutral, not pure black) as the dominant surface, **Amber Lux** (rich warm gold) as the singular accent used sparingly for CTAs, key numerals, and highlight moments.
- Theme: dark only — matches evening mobile-gaming context and the "Lux"/premium positioning.
- Feel: luxury skyline at dusk — buildings catching gold light against a dark city. Editorial confidence, not gamer-neon, not glassmorphism.
- Anti-references: generic SaaS waitlist templates (centered card, gradient blob background, purple-to-blue AI gradients), cartoonish mobile-game UI (rounded candy buttons, comic fonts), finance-dashboard sterility (data tables, cyan/green tickers).
- Motion: one well-choreographed load-in (staggered reveal of headline/city imagery), subtle ambient movement (slow parallax or glow drift), not busy micro-animation everywhere.

### Design Principles
1. Gold is earned, not everywhere — Amber Lux appears on the CTA, the wordmark, and 1-2 key accents per section; everything else stays in the Coco Noir neutral range.
2. Skyline-as-hero, not icon-as-hero — the city-builder concept should be felt architecturally (rooflines, verticality, light) rather than illustrated as a cute mascot.
3. Copy leads with the hook ("real market, no real money, all the stakes"), not feature lists — this is a pre-launch waitlist, not a product manual.
4. One primary action per screen: join the waitlist. Every other element supports that action.
5. Asymmetric, editorial layout over centered SaaS-card conventions.
